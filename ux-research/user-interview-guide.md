# Weekndr — User Interview Guide
*Target: Parents with children aged 2–14 | Session length: 45–60 min*

---

## Moderator Notes
- Start with open-ended discovery. Don't mention Weekndr until Section 3.
- Let the participant finish their thought before probing. Silence is fine.
- If a participant says "I just Google it" or "I ask friends" — always follow with "walk me through the last time you did that."
- Avoid leading language like "do you find it frustrating that…" — ask "how do you feel about…" instead.

---

## Screener (pre-interview)
Confirm before booking:
- [ ] Has at least one child between ages 2–14
- [ ] Plans at least one family weekend outing per month
- [ ] Primary or co-primary decision maker for weekend plans
- [ ] Not employed by a travel, events, or family activity company

---

## Section 1 — Context & Habits
*Goal: Understand their baseline weekend planning behavior and mental model.*

**Warm-up**
1. Tell me a bit about your family — how many kids, what ages, what's your week generally like?
2. How do your weekends typically look? Walk me through a recent Saturday.

**Planning behavior**
3. When do you usually start thinking about what to do that weekend — Friday night, Thursday, days ahead?
4. Who's involved in deciding what to do — just you, your partner, the kids?
5. How do you currently figure out what to do? Walk me through your process from "we need to do something" to "okay we're doing X."
6. What sources do you use — apps, websites, group chats, word of mouth?
7. How often does your plan actually happen as intended vs. change on the day?

---

## Section 2 — Frustrations & Goals
*Goal: Surface the real pain points and what success looks like for them — unprompted.*

**Frustrations**
8. What's the hardest part of planning a weekend activity with your kids?
9. Tell me about a time a plan completely fell apart — what happened, and how did it feel?
10. What do you wish you could just *not* have to think about when it comes to weekend planning?
11. Have you ever gotten somewhere and realized it wasn't what you expected — wrong for your kids' ages, too expensive, too far? What happened?
12. How do you handle it when the weather changes your plans last-minute?

**Goals & motivation**
13. What are you really trying to accomplish on a good family weekend — what does success feel like at the end of Sunday?
14. How important is budget to you when picking activities? How do you usually figure out what something will actually cost?
15. How much does your kids' specific age or stage factor into what you pick?
16. Do your kids have strong opinions about what they want to do? How do you balance that with what's actually practical?

---

## Section 3 — Concept Reaction (Weekndr)
*Goal: Get honest, unfiltered reactions to the product before showing the UI.*

**Concept description (read aloud):**
> "Imagine an app where you enter your location, your kids' ages and interests, and your budget for the weekend. Within seconds it generates a full Saturday and Sunday itinerary — specific activities, what each one costs including entry, parking and food, how long the drive takes, and a backup indoor plan in case the weather turns."

17. What's your immediate reaction to that?
18. What would make you trust a recommendation like this — or not trust it?
19. What's the first thing you'd want to double-check or verify before committing to the plan?
20. What's missing from that description that you'd want the app to also handle?
21. Can you think of a situation where this would be exactly what you need? And one where it wouldn't work for you?

**UI walkthrough** *(show the live app or screenshots — note reactions aloud)*
22. Take a few minutes to explore this. Think out loud as you go.
23. What's confusing or unexpected?
24. What would you want to change or add?
25. Is there anything here you'd never use?

---

## Section 4 — Feature Depth
*Goal: Identify gaps and prioritize enhancements.*

26. The app gives you a primary activity and an indoor backup for each day. How useful is that structure to you — or would you want more options?
27. The itinerary includes itemized costs (entry, parking, food). Is that level of detail useful, or is it overkill?
28. Drive time is built into the schedule. Does that change how you'd use the plan?
29. If the app remembered your family profile and past weekends — like "you did the science museum last month, here's something new" — would that matter to you?
30. Would you want to be able to share the itinerary with a co-parent, grandparent, or your kids? How?
31. Are there activity types that are totally off-limits for your family that you'd want to be able to block out?
32. If you could add one thing to this app that would make it a must-have for you, what would it be?

---

## Section 5 — Success Metrics & Willingness to Pay
*Goal: Understand how parents measure value and what the app would need to earn.*

33. If you used this every weekend for a month, how would you know it was actually working for you?
34. What would "this app saved my weekend" actually look like in practice?
35. How would you feel if this app stopped working and you had to go back to your old process?
36. Would you pay for this? If so, what would feel fair — and what would feel like too much?
37. Would you pay more for it if it also let you book the activities directly, or is planning enough?
38. Who else do you know who would benefit from something like this?

---

## Section 6 — Wrap-Up
39. Is there anything about your family's weekend planning that we haven't talked about but feels important?
40. What's the one thing you'd want the team building this app to know?

---

## Post-Interview Synthesis Tags
Rate each participant on (1–5) after the session:
- **Pain severity** — how acute is their planning frustration?
- **Tech comfort** — likelihood of adopting an AI-generated plan without friction
- **Budget sensitivity** — how central is cost to their decision-making
- **Retention signal** — how likely is repeat weekly use based on their habits?
- **WTP signal** — willingness to pay, and at what tier

---

## Hypotheses to Validate

| Hypothesis | Questions that test it |
|---|---|
| Parents experience decision fatigue by Friday that impairs weekend planning | Q8, Q10, Q3 |
| Unexpected costs (parking, food) are a top frustration | Q11, Q14, Q27 |
| Weather unpredictability is a recurring plan-breaker | Q12, Q26 |
| Parents distrust AI-generated plans for kids' activities | Q18, Q19 |
| Weekly-use retention requires memory/personalization | Q29, Q33 |
| Booking integration is not needed at MVP | Q37 |
| Age-appropriate filtering is core, not nice-to-have | Q15, Q21 |
