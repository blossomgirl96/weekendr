# Interview 05 — Priya N.
*Simulated participant | Conducted: 2026-05-03*

**Profile:**
- Married, 2 kids: Rohan (5), Ananya (8)
- Location: Lewis Center, OH (Columbus suburb, ~30 min from downtown)
- Occupation: Software engineer at a fintech company (remote)
- HHI: ~$105K (Priya + husband Vikram, who is a high school math teacher)
- Planning role: Co-primary; Priya researches, Vikram usually drives and helps execute
- Tech comfort: Very high — early adopter, builds side projects, has used multiple AI tools

---

## Section 1 — Context & Habits

**Tell me about your family.**

It's me, my husband Vikram, and two kids — Rohan is five, just finished pre-K, and Ananya is eight, third grade. We live in a suburb of Columbus — Lewis Center, which is very nice, very safe, but not exactly bursting with things to do. We chose it for the schools and the yard and the cost of living, and most of the time that trade-off makes sense. But on weekends I definitely feel the limitations. We're 25-30 minutes from downtown Columbus where most of the interesting things are.

Our families — both mine and Vikram's — are Indian. Culture matters to us. We try to expose the kids to Carnatic music, Indian art forms, Diwali events, that kind of thing. When I'm planning, I'm also thinking about whether there's something culturally meaningful we could do, not just "generic family activity."

**How do your weekends look?**

Saturday is our activity day. We try to do something that gets us all out — ideally to Columbus or at least to a larger park nearby. Sundays we often stay closer to home — temple, family cooking, low-key. But Saturday I plan intentionally.

Last Saturday we drove to COSI — the science center in Columbus. Both kids loved it. It's one of our reliable go-tos. But we've been there four or five times now. The kids still enjoy it but I feel like we're in a rut, and I'm not sure what else Columbus has that's comparable.

**When do you start planning?**

Usually by Wednesday. I'll do a search Wednesday night after the kids are in bed. Sometimes I save things to a running note in my phone. But the Columbus-area family activity content online is... thin. There are maybe three websites that cover it and they're not always up to date.

**How do you find activities?**

Google, primarily. Columbus Parent magazine sometimes. Facebook groups for Indian families in Columbus — that's actually really useful for culturally specific events. Reddit r/Columbus for local recommendations. I've also used ChatGPT, which is helpful for brainstorming but the Columbus-specific knowledge is inconsistent. It'll sometimes suggest places that don't exist or get the details wrong.

**How often do your plans work?**

Logistically, almost always. The bigger issue is that I often default to the same three or four places because I know they work. I'm not discovering new things.

---

## Section 2 — Frustrations & Goals

**What's the hardest part?**

Activity density. Columbus is a medium city. It's not New York or Chicago — there isn't infinite variety. When I've exhausted the obvious options, I hit a wall. I feel like I'm doing the same rotation every month, and that bothers me. The kids don't notice as much, but I notice. I feel like I'm not giving them a wide enough range of experiences.

The other thing is the drive. Anything worth doing is 30 minutes away. That doesn't sound like much but with a five-year-old, 30 minutes in the car is a commitment. If we get there and it's disappointing, the drive home is really miserable.

**A plan that fell apart?**

Last winter I found this ice sculpting event in downtown Columbus. It sounded magical — outdoor, artistic, something different. I didn't check the temperature. It was 12 degrees that day. Rohan lasted about ten minutes before he was in tears from the cold. We drove 35 minutes to spend ten minutes outside and then came home. Ananya was furious. Vikram was silent in a way that was worse than if he'd said something. I felt like the worst planner in the world.

**What do you wish you could stop thinking about?**

Whether the drive is worth it. That calculation is always there. I'm essentially asking: is this activity good enough to justify 30 minutes each way and the disruption to Rohan's afternoon routine? And I often can't know until we're already there.

**Unexpected cost surprises?**

Sometimes. COSI membership is worth it because we go often enough, but for one-off activities the total cost isn't always clear upfront. I'd like to know if an activity is going to be $30 or $80 for the four of us before I start getting the kids excited about it.

**Weather?**

Ohio weather is genuinely unpredictable. And it's not just rain — it's cold, it's icy, it's grey for months in winter. My indoor backup list gets a lot of use October through March. And my backup list is honestly too short.

**What are you really trying to accomplish?**

Two things. One: I want the kids to have real experiences — sensory, educational, creative. Not just screen time in a different location. Two: I want to feel like we're making the most of where we live. Columbus isn't a major metro, but it has things. I want to find those things rather than feel like we're missing out because we're not in a bigger city.

And honestly? I want Vikram to come home from a Saturday feeling like we did something right. He's a teacher — his weekdays are exhausting in a different way than mine. A good Saturday matters to our relationship.

**Budget?**

We're conscious of it. $60-80 for a one-time activity is the range I'm comfortable with. More than that and I feel like it needs to be something special. Memberships make sense for places we go more than three times a year.

**Ages and interests as a filter?**

Big factor. Rohan is five — he still needs age-appropriate framing, shorter duration, high physical engagement. Ananya at eight is much more capable and wants complexity. The good news is their interest overlap is better than it might be — both of them like animals, science, and any kind of hands-on making. That's helped.

## Section 3 — Concept Reaction

**[Concept read aloud]**

Oh, this is interesting. My first reaction is that this is the gap I've been filling manually with a combination of Reddit, Facebook groups, ChatGPT, and a lot of uncertainty. The structured output — two days, cost, drive time, backup — is exactly what I'd want, if the Columbus-area data is actually good.

That's my caveat. The question is whether it knows Columbus well enough to surface things beyond COSI, the Columbus Zoo, and Franklin Park Conservatory — which are the three things every generic "things to do in Columbus" list includes.

**What would make you trust it?**

If it surfaces things I haven't heard of but that turn out to be real and good. That's the trust signal. If I go to something the app recommended that I would never have found on my own, and it's great — that's when I become a believer. If it just tells me to go to COSI again, I'll assume it's as thin as everything else.

**What would you verify?**

Whether it's actually open and whether the age range is appropriate for a five-year-old. And for any outdoor activity in Ohio, the weather forecast. I have a weather app permanently in my brain now.

**What's missing?**

A cultural filter. I want to be able to say "include culturally diverse or Indian cultural events when available." The Columbus Indian community has events — Diwali melas, Bharatanatyam performances, cultural festivals. Those would never show up in a generic "family activity" search. If I could flag that as a preference, the app would immediately stand out from everything else I've used.

Also: a "worth the drive" signal. If the app knows I'm 30 minutes out, I want it to tell me "this is worth the drive because X" or suggest activities that cluster so I can do two things in Columbus in one trip rather than making a separate drive for each.

**Works well vs. doesn't?**

Works well: any Saturday when I want something beyond the rotation. Works less well: when we want to do specifically cultural events that require local community knowledge the app probably doesn't have. Also doesn't help with temple-related things or community events that aren't well-indexed online.

**[UI walkthrough]**

Clean and fast. I like the input structure. ...I'd want to put in "Columbus, OH — willing to drive 30-45 min." ...Interests field — I'd put animals, science, Indian culture. I'm curious if "Indian culture" actually changes the output or if it just ignores it. ...The cost breakdown is useful — especially for one-off Columbus trips where I don't always know if parking is free or $15. ...Indoor backup plan is essential for Ohio winters. ...I'd want to save multiple generated plans and compare before committing.

**What's confusing?**

Whether the app understands distance from a suburb vs. a city center. My "location" is Lewis Center but the activities are all in Columbus proper — does it search the right radius?

**What would you change?**

A drive radius selector, not just a city input. And a cultural preferences field that's distinct from general interests. And the ability to generate a "cluster itinerary" — two activities in the same part of Columbus so one drive = two things.

**Anything you'd never use?**

Nothing I'd never use, but I'd stop using it if the activity set was too thin for Columbus — if it kept cycling through the same five options.

## Section 4 — Feature Depth

**Primary + backup — enough?**

Yes. But I'd want the backup to account for Ohio winters specifically — "too cold to be outside" is as common for us as "raining." Not just "indoor if it rains."

**Cost breakdown?**

Very useful. Especially if it includes membership context — like "COSI membership pays off after 3 visits; single day entry is $22/adult."

**Drive time in schedule?**

Very important for us given the suburban-to-city dynamic. I'd want it to integrate with real-time traffic, not just average time. Columbus has construction that regularly adds 20 minutes on weekends.

**Memory and history?**

Critical. We've been to COSI, Columbus Zoo, Franklin Park Conservatory, and the Science Center many times each. If the app starts there, I lose faith. I want it to know what we've done and build outward from there.

**Sharing?**

I'd share with Vikram on Friday so he knows the plan and can help prep the kids. And sometimes with my mother-in-law if she's visiting.

**Blocklists?**

Anything that's more than 45 minutes away without being exceptional. And anything that would embarrass Ananya with her eight-year-old peers — she's at the age where she has opinions about not looking babyish.

**One must-have?**

Cultural event integration. If the app could pull in events from the South Asian cultural community in Columbus — even just an "Indian events near you" layer — it would be genuinely differentiated from everything else I've tried.

## Section 5 — Success Metrics & WTP

**How would you know it was working?**

If I'm consistently discovering things I didn't know about and the activities are actually good. And if the Saturday-to-Columbus drive feels worth it more often than not. Right now I'd say 60% of our planned Saturdays feel worth the effort. If this got me to 80%, I'd consider it a real improvement.

**"Saved my weekend"?**

Discovering a ceramic workshop near campus that Ananya loves, where Rohan can do a simple pinch pot beside her. Something specific, age-differentiated within one activity, within driving distance. That kind of find — which would have taken me two hours of research to surface on my own — in thirty seconds.

**If it disappeared?**

I'd feel it on winter weekends especially. October to March is when we struggle most. Summer and fall we're fine — parks and outdoor stuff is abundant. It's the grey months where I really need help.

**Would you pay?**

Yes. $8-12 a month. I'd want a trial period to evaluate whether the Columbus coverage is good enough — that's the main unknown.

**Booking?**

Useful if it could handle reservations for things that require them — some workshops book up. But not worth paying extra for.

**Who else?**

Immigrants and first-generation Americans in mid-sized cities. We're navigating American family culture and our own cultural identity simultaneously, and most family apps don't see us at all. If this app did, word would spread fast in those communities.

---

## Section 6 — Wrap-Up

**Anything else?**

The mid-sized city problem is real and underappreciated. Columbus is the 14th largest city in the US. It's not a small town. But every "things to do with kids" list treats it as an afterthought. The activity density isn't thin — it's just not surfaced well. An app that actually indexed Columbus, Indianapolis, Raleigh, Louisville — those mid-tier metros — would be doing something no one else has done.

**One thing for the team?**

Don't just build for New York and Los Angeles. The families in medium-sized cities who have limited activity options and rely more heavily on discovery tools are actually the more underserved and loyal audience. If it works in Columbus, people will tell each other. This kind of app lives or dies on word of mouth in tight communities.
