# Interview 03 — Derek O.
*Simulated participant | Conducted: 2026-05-03*

**Profile:**
- Stay-at-home dad, 3 kids: Maya (3), Caleb (6), Zoe (9)
- Location: Alpharetta, GA (Atlanta suburb)
- Occupation: Former operations manager; full-time primary caregiver since 2022
- HHI: ~$210K (wife, Dr. Adaeze O., is an OB/GYN — long and unpredictable hours)
- Planning role: Primary; wife is often unavailable on weekends due to on-call schedule
- Tech comfort: Moderate-high; organized, list-oriented, uses apps for most household logistics

---

## Section 1 — Context & Habits

**Tell me about your family.**

So I have three — Maya is three, Caleb is six, and Zoe just turned nine. I stay home full-time; my wife is an OB so her schedule is not always predictable. She's often on call weekends, which means I'm running the show solo a lot. I've basically become a professional family logistics coordinator at this point. I use a shared family calendar, I have lists for everything. I'm not disorganized. But even with all that, weekends are genuinely complicated because I have three kids at three very different stages.

**How do your weekends typically look?**

I try to plan something for Saturday that gets all three out of the house. Sunday I usually let be looser — maybe a neighborhood thing or one of the kids has a playdate. But Saturday I treat like a mission. Last Saturday we went to a nature center about 25 minutes away. Zoe was into the reptile exhibit, Caleb liked the birds, Maya just wanted to run around outside. It actually worked pretty well. But the week before that I tried a children's art workshop and Maya lasted eight minutes before she wanted to leave, and then I had to manage a meltdown in a quiet art space with a three-year-old while Zoe was in the middle of a project she cared about. It was a disaster.

**When do you start planning?**

I try to plan by Thursday. I have a loose system where I check a couple of Atlanta family event sites on Wednesday. But if Adaeze gets hit with an on-call that changes everything — then I'm replanning Thursday or Friday.

**How do you currently find activities?**

Atlanta Parent magazine's website has a good events calendar. I use Facebook groups for the Alpharetta area. I've tried ChatGPT a couple of times but the suggestions were either too generic or for places I knew weren't right for all three kids. I also have a mental inventory of about 15-20 places in the metro area that work for our family — I rotate through those when I don't have the energy to find something new.

**How often does your plan actually work?**

It works logistically most of the time. My real measure is whether all three kids were reasonably happy at the same time for most of the outing. That happens maybe fifty percent of the time. The other half, at least one kid is miserable for part of it.

---

## Section 2 — Frustrations & Goals

**What's the hardest part of planning with three kids at different ages?**

Finding the overlap. Maya is three — she needs physically accessible activities, short duration, lots of sensory input, she gets tired fast. Caleb is six — he's in that sweet spot where most "family" activities work. Zoe at nine wants more challenge, more complexity, and she'll sometimes visibly check out if she thinks something is "too little kid." So I'm looking for activities in this narrow zone where all three can be genuinely occupied and happy at the same time. That zone is smaller than you'd think.

The other thing is duration and pacing. Maya has maybe a two-hour active window. Zoe wants to stay longer. That mismatch creates conflict, and I'm always the one negotiating between them.

**A time a plan completely fell apart?**

The art workshop thing I mentioned. But also — we went to a big indoor trampoline park a few months ago. I thought it would be great because there were different zones for different ages. What I didn't know is that the toddler zone is not supervised and Maya was terrified of the older kids bouncing near her. She screamed for 20 minutes. Caleb and Zoe had a great time but I basically stood in a corner holding a crying three-year-old and couldn't participate. I left feeling like I'd failed everybody.

**What do you wish you could just stop thinking about?**

Whether it's going to work for all three at once. Every time I pick something I'm doing this five-way mental simulation: will Maya survive this? Will Caleb be bored? Will Zoe think this is beneath her? And then I'm also thinking: can I physically manage all three in this space alone if Adaeze doesn't come? Is there enough space to corral a three-year-old if she bolts? It's a safety and logistics calculation on top of the activity selection calculation.

**Unexpected cost surprises?**

Not really a budget concern for us, but I do notice when prices aren't transparent because I'm also computing the logistics cost — if I have to pay $30 parking on top of $60 entry I get frustrated not because of the money but because it feels like information I should have had upfront.

**Weather handling?**

I have a strong indoor backup system because I've learned the hard way. Georgia heat in summer basically eliminates outdoor activities by 11am. Rain is one thing, but heat is the bigger factor for us. I have a short list of indoor go-tos, but they're always the same five places.

**What are you really trying to accomplish?**

I want all three of them to have a genuinely good time for at least a few hours. If Zoe comes home and tells my wife about something she learned, or Caleb is physically exhausted in a good way, or Maya naps hard that afternoon — I call that a successful weekend. For me personally, I want to feel like I chose well. I made a call under uncertainty and it landed. There's a small satisfaction to that.

**Budget?**

Honestly secondary. What matters more is time efficiency. An activity that takes three hours to plan and two hours to execute is worse than an activity that takes 30 minutes to plan and four hours to execute, even if the latter costs more.

**Ages and stage as a filter?**

It's the primary filter. Before anything else. If something can't accommodate a three-year-old it's off the list, full stop.

## Section 3 — Concept Reaction

**[Concept read aloud]**

The thing that jumps out immediately is that it takes kids' ages — plural. Most tools I've used treat "family" as one unit. The fact that this takes specific ages means it might actually understand that a three-year-old and a nine-year-old are not interchangeable. If it delivers on that, this is something I'd use.

**What would make you trust it?**

If the output demonstrates it actually understands developmental differences. Not just "ages 3-9" — that's a cop-out. I want to see something like: "the nature trail has a paved path so stroller/young walker friendly, and a longer loop for the older kids." That's a recommendation that shows it understood the range. If it gives me a generic "great for all ages" description, I won't trust it.

**What would you verify first?**

Whether a three-year-old can actually participate, or whether she'll be bored or overwhelmed. I'd look up reviews specifically mentioning toddlers before committing. And I'd check whether the space is physically manageable solo — like is there a way I can keep visual contact with all three kids? That's something no app currently tells me.

**What's missing?**

Solo parent mode. I am often there alone with three kids. Some venues are not manageable solo with mixed ages — open water, multi-story buildings with no defined zones, things where you need to supervise closely and can't split attention. I need the app to understand that "can one adult manage this with three kids aged 3, 6, 9" is a variable. Also: weather-specific to the South. Summer heat is a plan-killer here more than rain.

**Works well vs. doesn't work?**

Works well: any Saturday where I don't have a plan by Thursday and I need one fast. Works less well: activities that require two adults, or anything where the three-year-old's participation is optional. I also wonder if it can handle Atlanta's specifics — not just national chains.

**[UI walkthrough]**

I like the age input — multiple kids, each with their own age. That's right. ...I want to put in all three ages and have the output reflect activities that the full range can do together. ...The cost breakdown is fine, parking info specifically is useful so I can think through logistics. ...I'd want the drive time to also factor in "with three kids in the car" — meaning the real departure time needs buffer for getting everyone in the car and strapped. ...The indoor backup is good. But for summer I'd want the backup to be "outdoor in the morning before it gets hot" rather than just "indoor if it rains."

**What's confusing?**

"Interests" for three kids. Whose interests? Do I put everyone's? What if they conflict?

**What would you change?**

A multi-kid interests field — one per child. Or at least a way to say "Zoe is into science, Caleb is into anything physical, Maya just needs stimulation and movement." Also a field for "solo parent" or "two adults" that changes the recommendations.

**Anything you'd never use?**

No, but I'm skeptical the activity database is deep enough for the Atlanta suburbs specifically. If it keeps recommending the Georgia Aquarium every time, I'll stop using it.

## Section 4 — Feature Depth

**One primary + one backup — is that enough?**

For me, two is right. What I'd add is a "quick option" — something within 10 minutes of home for the days when the plan falls apart at 8am and I just need a fast fix.

**Cost detail?**

Useful, especially parking. What I care more about is "what does this cost per kid" so I can do the mental accounting quickly.

**Drive time in schedule?**

Yes, and I'd add 20 minutes to whatever it says automatically. Loading three kids, making sure Maya didn't forget her shoes, re-locating Caleb who has wandered into the backyard — that's reality.

**Profile memory?**

Absolutely. I don't want to re-enter three kids' ages every weekend. And if it tracked "we've been to Chattahoochee Nature Center three times, suggest something different" — that would solve one of my real pain points.

**Sharing?**

I'd send it to Adaeze when she asks "what are you guys doing this weekend?" A clean shareable link would work.

**Blocklists?**

Immediately: anything that requires close supervision that's impossible solo (open water without defined zones), anything Zoe would consider "baby stuff," anything involving extended sitting (Caleb can't sit still for 45+ minutes).

**One must-have addition?**

Solo parent mode. A flag that tells the algorithm "this person is alone with multiple kids" and adjusts recommendations to venues that are physically manageable for one adult.

## Section 5 — Success Metrics & WTP

**How would you know it was working?**

If I open it Thursday, enter the kids' info, get a plan, and all three kids have a legitimately good time. My current hit rate is about 50% — if this gets me to 70-75% I would call that a significant improvement.

**"This saved my weekend" in practice?**

A Saturday where the plan worked for all three simultaneously. Where I didn't have to manage a crisis because one kid was miserable while the others were fine. That doesn't happen as often as I'd like.

**If it disappeared?**

I'd go back to my rotation of 15-20 known places. The rotation gets old but it's reliable. I'd miss the discovery function most.

**Would you pay?**

Yes. $10-12 a month is fine. Time is more valuable to me than money right now.

**Booking?**

Indifferent. I can book. The planning is the hard part.

**Who else?**

Every parent at Caleb's school who has more than two kids. And any family where one parent has irregular hours — which in the Alpharetta medical community is a lot of households.

---

## Section 6 — Wrap-Up

**Anything else?**

The physical space logistics thing I mentioned. "Is this manageable solo with a toddler" is a real variable that no tool addresses. Whether there are enclosed areas, whether there are quiet zones for meltdowns, whether there's a bathroom accessible without crossing the whole venue. These sound like details but when you're alone with a three-year-old they are the difference between a good outing and a nightmare.

**One thing the team should know?**

Families with wide age ranges between kids are underserved by every family activity product out there. Everything assumes your kids are close in age. We're not, and we're not unusual. Three-to-nine is a massive span. Build for that and you'll have very loyal users.
