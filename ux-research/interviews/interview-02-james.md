# Interview 02 — James T.
*Simulated participant | Conducted: 2026-05-03*

**Profile:**
- Married, 1 child: Emma (11)
- Location: Menlo Park, CA (South Bay / Palo Alto suburbs)
- Occupation: Product designer at a mid-size SaaS company
- HHI: ~$145K (James + Kelly, who is a pediatric nurse)
- Planning role: Co-primary with Kelly; James typically researches, Kelly approves
- Tech comfort: High — uses AI tools regularly; has used ChatGPT for trip planning before

---

## Section 1 — Context & Habits

**Tell me a bit about your family.**

Sure. It's me, my wife Kelly, and Emma who just turned 11. Kelly works rotating shifts at the hospital so our weekends are unpredictable — sometimes she's working Saturday, sometimes Sunday, sometimes both. So I end up being the main weekend planner a lot of the time. Emma is in that age where she's kind of transitioning out of "kid stuff" but isn't quite a teenager. She's outgrown a lot of things that used to work but she's not into things like concerts or restaurants yet. It's a weird middle zone.

**How do your weekends typically look?**

It honestly depends on Kelly's schedule. When Kelly's home we try to do something as a three of us — a day trip, a hike, something in the city. When she's working it's me and Emma, and Emma at 11 is... opinionated. She'll come with me but if she's not into it she's on her phone the whole time and it feels pointless.

The week before last, we did a kayaking thing in the bay — that was great, Emma loved it. But I probably spent 3–4 hours over two evenings researching that. I'm a designer, I research everything obsessively. That's partly a me problem.

**When do you typically start planning?**

Mid-week. I'll start a tab collection by Wednesday — you know, 12 browser tabs open. Then I'll try to narrow it down and run it by Emma to see if she vetoes anything. She vetoes about 70% of what I find.

**How do you currently find activities?**

I've used ChatGPT a few times, which works okay for generating lists but the outputs are generic and often outdated. I use Google extensively, TripAdvisor, Yelp, sometimes Atlas Obscura for unusual things. I subscribe to a couple of local "things to do" newsletters for the Bay Area. Kelly's more intuitive — she'll ask a colleague and get a great recommendation in 30 seconds.

**How often does your plan actually work out?**

Probably 80% of the time. Our main failure mode isn't logistics — it's Emma's engagement. We can get there and execute the plan fine, but if Emma's not feeling it, the vibe is off.

---

## Section 2 — Frustrations & Goals

**What's the hardest part of planning a weekend activity?**

Emma. She's old enough to have real preferences but she can't tell me what they are in a useful way. I'll say "what do you want to do this weekend?" and she'll say "I don't know." Then I'll suggest ten things and she'll say no to all of them. But then when we actually do something she ends up enjoying herself. There's this wall I have to get past. The kayaking was a veto initially — she said it sounded boring. She talked about it for two weeks after.

The other thing is that a lot of the "family activity" content online is designed for younger kids. If I search "things to do with kids in Bay Area" I get bounce houses and toddler play gyms. Emma is firmly not that demographic anymore but she's also not an adult. There's a genuine gap in the content for 10-13 year olds.

**Tell me about a time a plan fell apart.**

We tried to do this stargazing event at a local observatory — it was supposed to be great, Emma is into astronomy. I'd booked tickets weeks in advance. Day of, it was fully overcast. The observatory cancelled. We had nothing. Emma was devastated actually, which surprised me — she'd gotten attached to the plan. We ended up going to a movie. Not bad, but it felt like a waste of a Saturday.

**What do you wish you could not have to think about?**

Honestly? The research. I spend too much time on it. I enjoy it in theory but it's become a bit of a chore. I also wish I didn't have to guess what Emma will be into. She's at an age where I don't fully know anymore.

**Unexpected cost surprises?**

Not really an issue for us. Budget isn't the primary constraint.

**Weather?**

Yeah, the stargazing thing. But mostly we just pivot. We have more flexibility with a single kid and two incomes.

**What are you really trying to accomplish on a good family weekend?**

I want Emma to be present. Off the phone, actually in the experience. That's the victory condition. The specific activity is secondary. If I get Emma actually engaged and talking — that's the win. I also want Kelly to be de-stressed since she has hard weeks at the hospital. An easy, enjoyable weekend matters for her.

**How central is budget to your decision?**

It factors in but isn't a hard constraint. We'd cut a mediocre activity if it was too expensive but we'd pay for a great experience without much deliberation.

**Do your kids have strong opinions?**

Emma has strong opinions and a sophisticated veto power. But she doesn't generate ideas. She only reacts to mine. It's a very specific dynamic — I have to bring options, she filters. The problem is I'm doing all the sourcing.

## Section 3 — Concept Reaction

**[Concept read aloud]**

That's a cleaner version of what I've tried to assemble manually with ChatGPT. My immediate reaction is that the structure — specifically the two-day output with backup plans — is the thing that's missing from what ChatGPT gives me. ChatGPT gives me a list. It doesn't give me a schedule with drive times built in, and it definitely doesn't give me a backup for weather. I'd use this.

**What would make you trust it?**

Freshness of data. I've burned myself with ChatGPT recommendations that were for venues that had closed or changed. If I knew the activity data was updated regularly — like within the last month — I'd trust it more. Also, specificity. If it recommends something vague like "explore the waterfront" that's not a recommendation, that's a generic filler. I want specific: name, address, what makes it right for an 11-year-old, roughly how long to spend there.

**What would you verify before committing?**

Whether Emma would hate it or not. That's the one thing no app can tell me. Everything else — hours, cost, location — I can check. But I'd probably still have to do the Emma negotiation step before committing.

**What's missing?**

An age slider that goes up to tween/teen. And some ability to flag my kid's specific interests — Emma is into astronomy, drawing, mysteries, animals. I want the output to actually reflect those, not just "age-appropriate for 11." Also, can it generate a conversation hook I can use with her? Like "here's how to pitch this to your kid" — even a one-liner. That sounds silly but I genuinely would use it.

**A situation where this works, and one where it doesn't?**

Works perfectly: it's Thursday, Kelly just told me she's working Saturday and Sunday, and I need to figure out two full days with Emma. That happens regularly. Doesn't work well: when Emma has something specific she wants to do, or when we have family visiting and the activity is around them. Also maybe doesn't work if Kelly has something particular in mind — there's a co-planning dimension this doesn't solve.

**[UI walkthrough]**

Clean input. I like that it's fast. ...I'd want to be able to say "Emma is 11 and into: astronomy, drawing, animals." I don't see a freeform field for that. ...The cost breakdown is fine, not super relevant to me but I get why others would value it. ...The backup plan is good. I'd want to know what triggers it — is this a "if it rains" backup or a general alternative? ...I'd want to bookmark or save multiple generated itineraries and compare. Right now it feels like one shot — generate, take it or leave it.

**What's confusing?**

"Interests" is a single field — I'd want to put in multiple specific interests, not just one or two words.

**What would you change?**

The ability to regenerate or tweak one day without losing the other. And a way to mark "Emma approved" or "Emma vetoed" so it learns over time what she engages with.

**Anything you'd never use?**

The cost breakdown is low priority for me personally but I understand its value for other families.

## Section 4 — Feature Depth

**Primary + backup structure — enough options?**

For me, two options per day is right. I could see wanting a quick "regenerate this day" button if neither option clicks with Emma, but I don't want a full list to evaluate.

**Itemized costs?**

Fine but not essential for my situation. I could see this being the most important feature for families on tighter budgets.

**Drive time in the schedule?**

Yes — this is actually useful for us. We live in the Bay Area. Traffic is real. Knowing that something is 47 minutes on a Saturday morning vs. an hour and a half on Sunday afternoon changes the decision. If it could factor in Bay Area traffic patterns that would be impressive.

**Memory and past-activity history?**

Very important. Emma is 11 and she's been to every major Bay Area attraction twice already. If the app doesn't know what we've done, it's going to keep recommending the Exploratorium and we've been there four times. A "we've done this" log would be essential for us.

**Sharing the itinerary?**

I'd share it with Kelly so she knows what we're doing when she gets off shift. And maybe with Emma — showing her the plan in advance might help get her buy-in.

**Blocklists?**

I'd use this to exclude things Emma has explicitly said she finds "little kid." Bounce houses, face painting, petting zoos — she'd be mortified.

**One thing that would make it a must-have?**

Tween-specific filtering. If it genuinely understood the 10-13 demographic and gave me things Emma would actually engage with — not toddler stuff, not adult stuff — I'd become a regular user immediately.

## Section 5 — Success Metrics & WTP

**How would you know it was working after a month?**

If the activities it suggests result in Emma being genuinely engaged at least half the time. And if I'm spending less time researching. Right now I sink 3-4 hours into planning. If that's 30 minutes and the quality is the same or better, that's a clear win.

**What would "this app saved my weekend" look like?**

A Thursday afternoon where Kelly texts me "I'm working both days this weekend" and I open the app, enter Emma's current interests, get a Saturday and Sunday plan in 20 seconds, and it's actually something Emma ends up enjoying. That would be remarkable.

**How would you feel if it disappeared?**

I'd go back to my tabs-and-ChatGPT workflow. It would feel like a step backwards.

**Would you pay?**

Yes. $10-15 a month is reasonable for the time it would save me. I'd want to be confident in the data quality first though — I'd use a free trial seriously before committing.

**Booking integration?**

I already book through venue websites. But I'd pay slightly more if booking integration meant guaranteed availability confirmation — that's the thing that actually trips me up sometimes (showing up to a sold-out event).

**Who else would benefit?**

Kelly and I have a few friends with similarly-aged kids. They all have the same "what do we do this weekend" conversation every week. Also grandparents — my in-laws visit regularly and they always ask us to plan something "Emma would enjoy" and we scramble.

---

## Section 6 — Wrap-Up

**Anything we haven't covered?**

The social layer. Emma is at an age where activities with friends matter more than activities with parents. A feature that let me plan a weekend activity that could include one of her friends — like "plan something for 2 kids aged 11 that aren't related to each other" — would be interesting.

**One thing the team should know?**

The "family activity" category online completely ignores the 10-13 year old. Every product, every list, every recommendation assumes "family" means toddlers or elementary schoolers. If you nail that demographic you'd have very little competition.
