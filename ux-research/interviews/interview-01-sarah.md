# Interview 01 — Sarah M.
*Simulated participant | Conducted: 2026-05-03*

**Profile:**
- Single mom, 2 kids: Lily (4), Noah (7)
- Location: Chicago, IL (Logan Square neighborhood)
- Occupation: 3rd grade teacher
- HHI: ~$62K
- Planning role: Sole decision maker
- Tech comfort: Moderate — uses iPhone, Facebook groups, occasional Google

---

## Section 1 — Context & Habits

**Tell me a bit about your family — how many kids, what ages, what's your week generally like?**

So it's just me and my two — Lily's four, she just started pre-K, and Noah is seven, second grade. My weeks are... a lot. I'm a teacher so people think I have it easy but by Friday I am done. Like fully depleted. And then I have to switch into mom mode and figure out what we're doing Saturday and Sunday, and honestly that transition is one of the hardest parts of my week.

**How do your weekends typically look? Walk me through a recent Saturday.**

Okay so last Saturday — I woke up and I still had no plan. Which happens more than I want to admit. I'd been meaning to figure something out Thursday or Friday but I just didn't. So I'm sitting there at 8am, both kids are up, Noah's already bored, and I'm on my phone scrolling Facebook trying to find ideas. Someone had posted in the neighborhood moms group about this puppet theater thing at the library — but it was only for ages 5 and up, so Lily was too young. So then I go back to square one, spend another 30 minutes looking, and eventually we just went to the park and got pizza. Which is fine, we do that basically every other week.

**When do you usually start thinking about what to do that weekend?**

Honestly? Friday night. Sometimes Friday during my lunch break if I'm organized. I keep telling myself I'll plan on Wednesday but it never happens. The week just swallows everything.

**Who's involved in deciding?**

Mostly me. I'll ask Noah because he has opinions now, but Lily just goes with whatever. The hard part is Noah will veto things he doesn't want to do and then I have to start over.

**How do you currently figure out what to do?**

Facebook groups for local moms is my first stop. Then Google — like "things to do with kids in Chicago this weekend." Sometimes I check the Chicago Park District website. Occasionally Yelp if I'm looking for a specific type of thing. But it takes so long. I'll spend forty-five minutes and still not feel confident in what I picked.

**How often does your plan actually happen as intended?**

Maybe sixty percent of the time? Weather kills a lot of plans. Or I get there and it's crowded and the kids melt down. Or I underestimated how much it costs and I have to bail.

---

## Section 2 — Frustrations & Goals

**What's the hardest part of planning a weekend activity with your kids?**

The age gap between my two is really hard. Lily and Noah are in totally different places developmentally. Things that are perfect for a seven-year-old are either boring or overwhelming for a four-year-old. So I'm constantly doing this mental filter — is this too old for Lily? Will Noah think this is babyish? It narrows everything down to this tiny overlap zone and I never feel like I'm fully in that zone.

**Tell me about a time a plan completely fell apart.**

Oh god. Last fall — I'd planned this whole apple picking day. I'd found this orchard about 45 minutes away, I was so excited. I didn't check the website closely enough and it turned out they'd sold out of their U-pick slots for the weekend months in advance. We drove out there and just... couldn't do it. Lily cried, Noah was furious. We drove home. I felt like a complete failure. And I'd spent two hours of my Friday night researching and getting excited about this plan.

**What do you wish you could just not have to think about?**

Whether it's actually age-appropriate for both of them. And cost. I'm always doing math in my head. Like okay entry is X, parking is probably Y, and they're gonna want food, so that's another Z. I want someone to just tell me the total upfront so I can decide yes or no without having to go dig through a website.

**Have you ever gotten somewhere and realized it wasn't what you expected?**

Yes. There's this children's museum I took them to based on a friend's recommendation — she has older kids. It was fine for Noah but Lily was really too small for most of the exhibits. I paid like $45 to get in for both of them and Lily spent the whole time at the water table in the toddler section, which we have at her preschool for free. I felt like I wasted money and I should have known better, but how would I have known?

**How do you handle weather changes last-minute?**

I panic a little. I have like two or three indoor backups in my head — the library, the children's museum, this indoor play gym — but they're always the same ones and we've done them so many times. Honestly if it rains on a Saturday I feel this dread because I know I'm going to have to scramble and probably land on something we've already done a hundred times.

**What are you really trying to accomplish on a good family weekend?**

I want my kids to feel like they did something. Like the weekend had a shape to it, not just two days of TV and the same park. Noah especially needs stimulation or he gets cranky. For me, I want to feel like a good mom. That sounds pathetic but it's true — a good weekend where we did something real makes me feel like I'm doing it right even though I'm doing it alone. And I want to not be exhausted by the planning. I want the planning to take five minutes, not two hours.

**How important is budget?**

Very. I have maybe $80-100 for a weekend activity, total. That's entry, any food, parking. And in Chicago, parking alone can be $20-30, which kills the budget immediately if I'm not careful. I've gotten to places and realized I'm going to blow my whole budget on parking and entry before we've eaten anything, and then I either have to cut corners on food or feel guilty.

**How much does your kids' age or stage factor in?**

It's like the number one thing. Age is the filter I apply before anything else. If something isn't right for a four-year-old and a seven-year-old at the same time, it's off the list, doesn't matter how cool it is.

**Do your kids have strong opinions?**

Noah has very strong opinions. He went through a phase of only wanting to go to Lego things and science museums. Lily doesn't care much yet — she just wants to be around Noah and do what he does. But Noah will look at me and go "that sounds boring" and it genuinely deflates me.

## Section 3 — Concept Reaction

**[Concept read aloud]**

*[Pause — 4 seconds]*

Okay that sounds... really good. My immediate reaction is where was this a year ago. The indoor backup plan thing especially — that is such a specific pain for me. Like I didn't know I needed someone to just tell me "here's what you're doing Saturday, here's the rain plan for Sunday, this is how much it'll cost, this is how long the drive is."

**What would make you trust a recommendation like this — or not trust it?**

I'd need to know it actually knows the difference between a four-year-old and a seven-year-old. Not just "family-friendly" — that label means nothing. I've been burned by "family-friendly" things that were really for kids 6 and up. If I put in Lily's age and it still recommends something that's way over her head, I'd stop trusting it immediately. I'd also want to know if the hours and prices are current. Not from six months ago.

**What's the first thing you'd want to verify before committing?**

That it's actually open. And the real cost. I've had apps recommend places that have permanently closed, or had prices that were way off. I'd go to the actual website to double-check those two things before I told the kids we were going.

**What's missing from that description?**

Can it tell me if it's going to be crowded? Like if it's a holiday weekend and the place is going to be a nightmare, I'd rather know. Also — is there food nearby or at the venue? I don't want to drive 30 minutes somewhere and then realize there's no food for miles when the kids get hungry.

**A situation where this would be exactly what you need, and one where it wouldn't work?**

Exactly what I need: Friday night, I'm exhausted, I have no plan, and I just need someone to tell me what to do tomorrow. That's 80% of my weekends. Where it wouldn't work: if I wanted to do something specific that we've already planned, like a birthday party or visiting a friend. I don't need it when I already know what we're doing.

**[UI walkthrough — think aloud]**

Okay the input form is really clean. I like that it asks for kids' ages separately, not just "how many kids." ...Oh, the itemized breakdown is great. Entry, parking, food — yes, this is exactly the format I was describing. ...Hmm, I'm not sure what "interests" means for a four-year-old. Like how specific do I get? Animals? Water? Is there like a dropdown or do I just type? ...The backup plan is right there without me having to ask for it. I love that. ...I wish I could see the Saturday and Sunday plans at the same time on one screen. Having to scroll feels like extra work.

**What's confusing or unexpected?**

The "interests" input. I don't know what to put for Lily. And I'm not sure if what I type actually changes the output meaningfully or if it gives the same suggestions regardless.

**What would you change or add?**

A "we've been here before" button to exclude places we've already done. And I'd want it to remember my kids' ages so I don't re-enter them every time.

**Is there anything you'd never use?**

Not that I can see. Everything here seems useful.

## Section 4 — Feature Depth

**Primary + indoor backup structure — useful, or do you want more options?**

One primary and one backup per day is perfect for me. I don't want five options — that's the whole problem I have now, too many options and I can't decide. Just tell me what to do and give me an out if it rains. Done.

**Itemized cost breakdown — useful or overkill?**

Not overkill at all — this is the feature I didn't know I needed most. I want to know total cost upfront. I'd love if it also told me whether parking is free or paid and approximately how much.

**Drive time embedded in the schedule — does that change how you'd use it?**

Yes. I have a thing about being late with kids. If I know I need to leave by 9:15 to get there by 10 with a 4-year-old who needs a snack before we go, I can actually plan for that. Drive time as part of the schedule, not just a link, is different.

**Family profile memory and past-weekend history?**

That would be huge. Right now the thing I dread most is paying $45 to do something we've already done six times. If it remembered "you went to the science museum in March, here are alternatives" I would trust it so much more. That feature alone would make me pay for the app.

**Sharing the itinerary?**

I'd share with my mom who sometimes comes along. Or with Noah so he feels like he had input. A shareable link or a screenshot would be enough.

**Activity blocklists?**

Yes — I'd block anything involving a lot of walking for Lily's age (she gets tired fast), anything that's primarily for one age and not the other, and honestly I'd put in "no trampoline parks" because I find them chaotic and I can't watch two kids at once in there.

**One thing that would make this a must-have?**

If it remembered my family and I could just open it and press "plan this weekend" without re-entering anything. One tap. Done.

## Section 5 — Success Metrics & WTP

**If you used this every weekend for a month, how would you know it was working?**

If I stopped spending Friday nights stressed out trying to figure out what to do. That's the metric. If planning goes from 45 minutes to 5 minutes. And if the activities are actually good — like the kids come home happy, not "that was boring."

**What would "this app saved my weekend" look like?**

Last Saturday, I spent two hours planning and we ended up at the same park we always go to. If the app had been there I would have just opened it Friday afternoon, entered our info, and had a plan in 30 seconds. That's the version of Saturday I want.

**How would you feel if it stopped working and you had to go back to your old process?**

Genuinely annoyed. If it became part of my routine, losing it would feel like losing something I relied on.

**Would you pay for it?**

Yes. Honestly I'd pay for this even on my budget. If it does what it says, $5-8 a month feels worth it. I spend more than that on a single coffee. I wouldn't pay $15+ though — that's getting into "do I actually need this" territory for me.

**Booking integration — would you pay more for that?**

Maybe if it was seamless. But I'm used to going to the venue website and buying tickets there. I wouldn't pay extra specifically for booking unless it also guaranteed availability. The planning is the hard part for me, not the booking.

**Who else would benefit from this?**

Every single parent I know. And honestly any parent who has more than one kid with an age gap.

---

## Section 6 — Wrap-Up

**Anything about your family's weekend planning we haven't talked about?**

The energy thing. Some weekends I have more bandwidth than others. It would be cool if I could set an "energy level" for the weekend — like high energy (we're up for an adventure) vs. low energy (chill, close to home, nothing too intense). The app right now seems like it always outputs a full day plan, but sometimes I just want a half-day easy thing.

**One thing you'd want the team to know?**

Single parents are doing all of this alone. There's no backup person to hand this off to. If this app takes the planning off my plate for even one weekend a month, that is real. Make sure it actually knows what a four-year-old can handle. That's the thing I'm not sure any app gets right.
