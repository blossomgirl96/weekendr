## How to read this

Each cluster groups observations that share an underlying theme. Sub-clusters identify tighter patterns within the cluster. Each observation is tagged with the participant(s) who expressed it and whether it was **unprompted (U)** or **prompted (P)** — unprompted signals carry more weight.

# CLUSTER A — The Planning Burden

*How parents currently plan, what it costs them, and where it breaks down.*

---

### A1 — Planning is expensive in time and energy

- "I spend 45 minutes and still don't feel confident in what I picked." **S (U)**
- "I probably spent 3–4 hours over two evenings researching the kayaking thing." **J (U)**
- "I try to plan by Thursday but the week just swallows everything." **S (U)**
- "I check 3–4 platforms simultaneously before I land on something." **P (U)**
- "Wednesday night after the kids are in bed — that's my planning window." **P (U)**
- "I have a reminder in my calendar the Thursday before they come." **M (U)**

### A2 — Planning happens at lowest-energy moment

- "By Friday I am done. Fully depleted. And then I have to switch into mom mode." **S (U)**
- "Residual decision fatigue from the work week hits Friday night planning hardest." **S (U)**
- "Kelly has hard weeks at the hospital — a smooth Saturday matters for her too." **J (U)**
- "I try to plan mid-week but it's still happening when I'm tired." **P (U)**

### A3 — Current tools are fragmented and imprecise

- "I use Facebook groups, Google, ChatGPT, Yelp — none of them talk to each other." **S (U)**
- "ChatGPT gives me lists but they're generic and sometimes outdated." **J (U)**
- "ChatGPT's Columbus knowledge is inconsistent — sometimes suggests places that don't exist." **P (U)**
- "Atlanta family activity content online is three websites that aren't always current." **D (U)**
- "Every source gives me a starting point, not a plan." **M (U)**

### A4 — Parents fall back on a known rotation

- "I have a mental inventory of 15–20 places that work — I rotate through them." **D (U)**
- "We've been to COSI four or five times. The kids still enjoy it but I feel like we're in a rut." **P (U)**
- "If it rains I know I'm going to land on something we've done a hundred times." **S (U)**
- "The rotation gets old but it's reliable." **D (U)**
- "I've used up the obvious ones — Natural History Museum more times than I can count." **M (U)**

### A5 — The plan often survives logistics but fails experience

- "The plan works logistically about 80% of the time. Emma's engagement is the variable." **J (U)**
- "It works logistically. Whether all three are happy at the same time — that's 50%." **D (U)**
- "I'd say 60% of Saturdays feel worth the effort we put in." **P (U)**
- "Aiden is somewhere between 40–60% genuinely engaged depending on what we do." **M (U)**

# CLUSTER B — The Age Problem

*The central planning constraint: finding activities that work across multiple ages simultaneously.*

---

### B1 — Multi-age overlap is the core constraint

- "I'm looking for activities in this narrow zone where a 3-year-old and a 9-year-old can both be genuinely occupied. That zone is smaller than you'd think." **D (U)**
- "Things that are perfect for a seven-year-old are either boring or overwhelming for a four-year-old." **S (U)**
- "I need something Jordan can engage with at nine AND Aiden can engage with at twelve." **M (U)**
- "If something can't accommodate a three-year-old, it's off the list. Full stop." **D (U)**
- "Their interest overlap is actually decent — both like animals and science. That's helped." **P (U)**

### B2 — "Family-friendly" is a distrusted label

- "Family-friendly means nothing. I've been burned by that label." **S (U)**
- "I want specific language — not 'great for all ages.' That's a cop-out." **D (U)**
- "If I see 'family-friendly' and it's clearly for 5–8 year olds, I stop trusting the source." **J (U)**
- "I want to see something like 'paved path for young walkers, longer loop for older kids' — that shows it understood the range." **D (U)**

### B3 — The tween dead zone (ages 10–13)

- "The family activity category completely ignores 10–13 year olds." **J (U)**
- "Emma's outgrown a lot of things that used to work but she's not into adult things yet. It's a weird middle zone." **J (U)**
- "Aiden needs to not feel like the activity is for little kids. That filter is very real." **M (U)**
- "Searching 'things to do with kids in Bay Area' returns bounce houses and toddler play gyms. Emma is not that demographic." **J (U)**
- "Zoe at nine visibly checks out if she thinks something is too little kid." **D (U)**
- "If it keeps giving me things targeted at 5–8 year olds, I'll stop using it." **M (P)**

### B4 — Age minimums as a hidden risk

- "We drove to the escape room. They wouldn't let Jordan in — she was nine, minimum was twelve." **M (U)**
- "I took them to a workshop that was listed as all-ages. Maya lasted eight minutes." **D (U)**
- "I paid $45 for a children's museum — Lily spent the whole time at the toddler water table." **S (U)**
- "Age minimum is now the first thing I check on any venue. Always." **M (U)**

### B5 — Pacing mismatch between siblings

- "Maya has maybe a two-hour active window. Zoe wants to stay longer. That mismatch creates conflict." **D (U)**
- "A five-year-old can do a 30-minute drive if I know it's worth it. A bad destination makes the drive home really miserable." **P (U)**
- "Lily gets tired fast — I'd block anything involving a lot of walking." **S (P)**

# CLUSTER C — Trust & Verification

*What makes parents trust a recommendation, and what they do to check.*

---

### C1 — Specificity is the trust signal

- "Generic suggestions like 'explore the waterfront' are not recommendations — they're filler." **J (U)**
- "I want: name, address, what makes it right for an 11-year-old, how long to spend there." **J (U)**
- "If the language shows it understood the age range — that earns trust." **D (U)**
- "If it surfaces something I never would have found on my own and it's great — that's when I become a believer." **P (U)**
- "I want Columbus-specific recs, not 'visit a local park.'" **P (U)**

### C2 — Data freshness is an unresolved concern

- "I've burned myself with ChatGPT recommendations for venues that had closed." **J (U)**
- "I'd want to know the data was updated within the last month." **J (U)**
- "Columbus activity sites aren't always up to date. I've shown up to things that were already over." **P (U)**
- "I'd still check the venue website to confirm hours and price before telling the kids." **S (P)**

### C3 — Verification is inevitable — the question is how much

- "I'd still go to the venue website to double-check it's open and confirm cost." **S (P)**
- "Everything except whether Aiden will engage — that I can check myself." **M (U)**
- "I look up reviews specifically mentioning toddlers before committing." **D (P)**
- "Emma negotiation is still my job — no app can do that." **J (U)**

### C4 — One bad experience breaks trust permanently

- "I drove 45 minutes to the orchard. Sold-out U-pick slots. Lily cried. I felt like a failure." **S (U)**
- "Escape room wouldn't let Jordan in. Aiden said he didn't want to go without her. We stood on the sidewalk." **M (U)**
- "Ice sculpting at 12 degrees. Rohan lasted ten minutes. Vikram was silent in a way that was worse than if he'd said something." **P (U)**
- "We showed up to the trampoline park. Maya was terrified. I stood in a corner holding a crying three-year-old." **D (U)**

# CLUSTER D — Cost & Budget

*How financial constraints shape planning behavior and what transparency means to parents.*

---

### D1 — Budget ceiling is hard for lower-income parents

- "I have maybe $80–100 for a weekend activity, total. Entry, food, parking." **S (U)**
- "In Chicago, parking alone is $20–30, which kills the budget immediately." **S (U)**
- "I have an $80 ceiling. I can do one meaningful paid activity per weekend." **M (U)**
- "I can't do two expensive things. One main paid thing, one free thing." **M (U)**

### D2 — Hidden costs are an emotional trigger

- "I've gotten to places and realized I'm going to blow my whole budget on parking before we've eaten." **S (U)**
- "I need to know the total before I get the kids excited about it." **M (U)**
- "Cost wasn't the issue — it was that I didn't have the number before I made the decision." **D (U)**
- "The COSI membership makes sense because we go often enough. One-offs are less predictable." **P (U)**

### D3 — Cost transparency is a trust and respect signal

- "Tell me the total upfront so I can decide yes or no without digging through a website." **S (U)**
- "73% of parents cite affordability. The itemized breakdown is a direct response to that anxiety." *(synthesis)*
- "Parking info specifically — so I can think through the logistics." **D (P)**
- "Membership value context — 'pays off after 3 visits' — would help me decide faster." **P (P)**

### D4 — Higher-income parents value transparency for different reasons

- "It's not about money — it's about information I should have had upfront." **D (U)**
- "Cost isn't the constraint. Time is. An activity that takes 3 hours to plan is worse than one that costs more." **D (U)**
- "I'd pay for quality without much deliberation. But the data better be good." **J (U)**

# CLUSTER E — Weather & the Backup Plan

*How weather disrupts plans and what parents do — or wish they could do — about it.*

---

### E1 — Weather failure is universal and recurring

- "Weather kills a lot of plans. Maybe 40% of the time something changes because of it." **S (U)**
- "We booked the stargazing event weeks in advance. Day of — fully overcast, cancelled." **J (U)**
- "Ice sculpting at 12 degrees — I didn't check the temperature." **P (U)**
- "Ohio weather is genuinely unpredictable October through March." **P (U)**
- "Georgia heat in summer eliminates outdoor activities by 11am — more often than rain." **D (U)**

### E2 — The proactive backup plan is universally valued

- "If it rains I dread it — I'm going to scramble and land on something we've done a hundred times." **S (U)**
- "I didn't know I needed someone to just tell me 'here's the plan, here's the rain plan.'" **S (U)**
- "The indoor backup plan as a default output — that's the thing that jumps out." **D (U)**
- "Getting a primary + backup in one generation without having to ask is novel." **M (U)**

### E3 — "Indoor if raining" is a floor, not a ceiling

- "Georgia heat is the plan-killer, not rain. The backup needs to account for that." **D (U)**
- "In Ohio, 'too cold to be outside' is as common as 'raining.'" **P (U)**
- "'Indoor' in NYC means hundreds of options — I still need it to be specific." **M (U)**
- "Sometimes I want a 'low energy' backup, not just an indoor one." **S (P)**

### E4 — Current backup lists are thin and stale

- "My indoor backup list gets a lot of use but it's too short." **P (U)**
- "I have two or three indoor backups — they're always the same ones." **S (U)**
- "I've used up the obvious indoor options. Natural History Museum is maxed out." **M (U)**
- "I have a short list of indoor go-tos but they're the same five places every time." **D (U)**

# CLUSTER F — Memory & Personalization

*What parents want the app to remember and why it matters for retention.*

---

### F1 — Rotation fatigue is universal

- "I've been to COSI four or five times. I feel like we're in a rut." **P (U)**
- "The science museum again. We've been there four times." **J (P)**
- "We've done the same park, same children's museum, same indoor play gym cycle for a year." **S (U)**
- "My rotation of 15–20 places is reliable but it gets old. Discovery is the gap." **D (U)**

### F2 — Profile memory is the clearest paid feature trigger

- "If I could just open it and press 'plan this weekend' without re-entering anything — that's the version I want." **S (U)**
- "I don't want to re-enter three kids' ages every weekend." **D (U)**
- "If it remembered 'you went to the science museum in March, here are alternatives' — I'd trust it so much more." **S (U)**
- "That feature alone would make me pay for the app." **S (U)**
- "We've been to every major Bay Area attraction twice already. Without a history, it'll just repeat them." **J (U)**

### F3 — Engagement tracking, not just visit tracking

- "I'd want it to track what Aiden reacted well to — not just what we've been to." **M (U)**
- "Over time I want it to learn that Aiden engages with making/building, not passive museum experiences." **M (U)**
- "Mark 'Emma approved' or 'Emma vetoed' so it learns her pattern." **J (U)**
- "If the app knew Zoe is into science, Caleb is physical, Maya needs movement — that's the version worth having." **D (U)**

### F4 — Per-child interest profiles, not family-level

- "Whose interests do I put in — everyone's? What if they conflict?" **M (U)**
- "I'd want one interests field per child." **D (P)**
- "Rohan likes animals. Ananya likes science and art. If I have to collapse that into one field I lose something." **P (U)**
- "Emma is into astronomy, drawing, mysteries, animals. I want all of that to shape the output." **J (P)**

# CLUSTER G — Emotional Stakes

*What's really driving the planning effort — the emotional "why" behind the behavior.*

---

### G1 — Planning is a proxy for being a good parent

- "A good weekend where we did something real makes me feel like I'm doing it right even though I'm doing it alone." **S (U)**
- "I want to feel like I chose well. I made a call under uncertainty and it landed." **D (U)**
- "I felt like the worst planner in the world." **P (U)** *(re: ice sculpting incident)*
- "I felt like a complete failure." **S (U)** *(re: orchard incident)*

### G2 — The custody parent carries heightened pressure

- "These two days need to count. The clock is always ticking." **M (U)**
- "I'm building a bank of memories in forty-eight hours every two weeks." **M (U)**
- "If I have a bad weekend I can't make it up the next one — those two days are gone." **M (U)**
- "Every-other-weekend custody means I can't afford many 'meh' Saturdays." **M (U)**

### G3 — Presence and engagement are the real success metric

- "I want Emma to be present. Off the phone, actually in the experience. That's the victory condition." **J (U)**
- "If Aiden voluntarily talks about the activity afterward — that's the metric." **M (U)**
- "If Zoe tells my wife about something she learned, or Caleb is exhausted in a good way — I call that a win." **D (U)**
- "I want memories that are specific — 'remember the underground art thing in Dumbo,' not 'remember when we watched TV.'" **M (U)**

### G4 — Weekend quality extends to the adult relationship

- "A good Saturday matters to our relationship — Kelly has hard weeks." **J (U)**
- "I want Vikram to come home from Saturday feeling like we did something right." **P (U)**
- "The planning stress isn't just about the activity — it's about not having a partner to hand it off to." **S (U)**

# CLUSTER H — Unmet Needs & Feature Gaps

*Features not in the current product that participants raised unprompted.*

---

### H1 — Solo parent mode

- "Some venues are not manageable solo with mixed ages — I need the app to understand that." **D (U)**
- "Can one adult manage this with three kids aged 3, 6, 9?" — no app answers this." **D (U)**
- "Physical containment, supervision requirements, bathroom proximity — these are survival details when you're alone." **D (U)**
- "I'd flag this solo and it should filter out open water, multi-story unenclosed spaces." **D (P)**

### H2 — Transportation mode (transit vs. driving)

- "The parking info is meaningless to me. I don't have a car." **M (U)**
- "No subway, no activity — but apps don't know that." **M (U)**
- "If it gave me '30 min by subway from Crown Heights' that would be genuinely useful." **M (P)**
- "The car-centric UX fails NYC and Chicago users silently." *(synthesis)*

### H3 — Geographic depth for mid-sized cities

- "The question is whether it knows Columbus well enough beyond COSI, the Zoo, and Franklin Park Conservatory." **P (U)**
- "If it just tells me to go to COSI again I'll assume it's as thin as everything else." **P (U)**
- "Columbus is the 14th largest city in the US and every family activity list treats it as an afterthought." **P (U)**
- "Medium-sized cities — Columbus, Indianapolis, Raleigh, Louisville — these families are more underserved and more loyal." **P (U)**

### H4 — Suburban drive logic

- "Is this worth a 30-minute drive? That calculation is always present." **P (U)**
- "A drive radius selector, not just a city input." **P (P)**
- "Cluster itinerary — two activities in the same part of Columbus so one drive covers both." **P (P)**
- "If it knew I was 30 minutes out and told me 'worth the drive because X,' I'd trust that signal." **P (U)**

### H5 — Cultural and community events

- "A cultural filter — include Indian cultural events when available." **P (U)**
- "The Columbus Indian community has events. They'd never show up in a generic search." **P (U)**
- "If this did cultural events, word would spread fast in those communities." **P (U)**
- "We try to expose the kids to Carnatic music, Diwali events — it's not just generic family activity." **P (U)**

### H6 — Energy/intensity mode

- "Some weekends I have more bandwidth than others. I want to set an 'energy level.'" **S (U)**
- "High energy: we're up for an adventure. Low energy: chill, close, nothing intense." **S (U)**
- "The app seems like it always outputs a full day plan. Sometimes I just want a half-day easy thing." **S (U)**

### H7 — Diverging sibling interest bridging

- "Jordan loves animals/nature. Aiden is into tech and gaming. If the app averages those out I'll get something neither of them wants." **M (U)**
- "Finding activities that accidentally work for both kids — those are rare. If the app could surface them, it'd be valuable." **M (U)**
- "I want the output to bridge them, not average them." **M (P)**

### H8 — "Pitch to your kid" framing

- "A one-liner I can use with Emma — 'here's how to pitch this to your kid.'" **J (U)**
- "That sounds silly but I genuinely would use it. Emma vetoes before we arrive." **J (U)**
- "Aiden goes along if he buys into it first. If I show him the plan ahead of time it sometimes works." **M (U)**

---

### I2 — Emotional outcome, not logistics

- "All three kids having a genuinely good time at the same time." **D (U)**
- "Kids come home happy, not 'that was boring.'" **S (U)**

### I3 — The feeling at end of Sunday

- "The weekend had a shape to it — not just two days of TV and the same park." **S (U)**
- "Good Saturday + good Sunday = feeling like we did something real." **M (U)**
- "Vikram comes home from Saturday feeling like we did something right." **P (U)**
